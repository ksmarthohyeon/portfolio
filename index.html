Artist statement

When an animal is designed to save our lives, what do we owe it?

Based inSuwon, Korea
Working inWatercolour, acrylic, coloured pencil, hanji and mixed-media sculpture, video
SeriesEight works, 2025 — 2026, on animals, boundaries and human progress

As an artist, I am interested in lives that are often overlooked or sacrificed in the name of human progress.

The series opens with a world before humans drew clear boundaries between ourselves, animals, nature and the places we built, and moves through the places where those boundaries are felt most sharply: the aquarium tank, the classroom, the living room furnished from the animals on the television, the laboratory where a pig is rewritten to carry a human heart.

I work in whatever material the idea needs. Faux fur and yarn rise off a coloured-pencil drawing so that luxury becomes tactile. Real sand and gold paint are mixed into modelling paste for a shore that invites touch. Hanji, traditional Korean mulberry paper, is stretched over rattan so a body can read as both fragile and resilient. Slow hand-stitching stands for care, the opposite of the clinical glove.

I am not interested in villainy. Most of us live in indifference, and I would rather show that plainly, then suggest that rebuilding our relationship with nature can begin with something as small as a child reaching through a broken wall.
